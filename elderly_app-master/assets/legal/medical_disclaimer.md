# Medical disclaimer

Version {{legal_version}} · Effective {{effective_date}}

Please read this carefully. It explains what Elderly Care can and cannot do for your health.

## In an emergency

**If you or someone else may be in danger, call for help directly. Do not wait for the App.**

- From any mobile phone: **112**
- Ambulance and fire: **10177**
- Police: **10111**

The App's "I need help" button only opens your phone's own Phone and Messages apps. It does not contact emergency services, a call centre or anyone else by itself, and it cannot tell whether help is on its way.

## The App is not a doctor

- Elderly Care is a personal organiser. It helps you remember your medicines and appointments, write down your health readings and contact the people who look after you.
- It does **not** give medical advice, a diagnosis or treatment, and nothing in the App should be understood that way.
- It is **not** a medical device. It has not been tested, registered or approved by the South African Health Products Regulatory Authority (SAHPRA) or any other health authority.
- No doctor, nurse or pharmacist looks at the information you enter. Nobody is monitoring you through the App.

## Medicines

- Take your medicines exactly as your doctor or pharmacist told you, and as written on the label and package insert. If the App and your prescription ever differ, **follow your prescription**.
- The App only shows what you entered. It does not check whether a medicine, dose or time is correct, safe for you, or safe to take with your other medicines, food or alcohol. Ask your pharmacist about interactions.
- Never change, stop, double or skip a dose because of something in the App. If you missed a dose, follow the instructions on the package insert or ask your pharmacist or doctor what to do.
- Reminders can be late or missing (see "Reminders can fail" below). The App is a helper, not a guarantee.
- "Doses left" is only a count based on what you tapped. Check your actual supply and order repeats in good time.

## Health readings and charts

- Readings are only as accurate as the device you measured with and the numbers you typed in.
- The tips shown in the health tracker (for example about blood pressure, blood sugar or sleep) are general information for adults. Your own target values may be different. Ask your doctor what is right for you.
- The charts show what you entered. They cannot tell whether a change is dangerous, and they do not raise alarms.
- **Seek medical help straight away** if you have warning signs such as chest pain, difficulty breathing, sudden weakness or numbness on one side, slurred speech, a severe headache, confusion, fainting, a very high or very low blood sugar or blood pressure reading, or if you feel very unwell. Do not wait to see a trend in the App.

## Summaries you share

- Health summaries (message or PDF) are made from the information you entered and say so. Your doctor should treat them as notes from you, not as clinical records or test results.
- Check a summary before you send it, and make sure you send it to the right person.

## Reminders can fail

Your phone controls whether and when reminders appear. They can be delayed, silenced or blocked by battery saving, "Do not disturb", silent mode, a flat or dying battery, the phone being switched off or restarted, system updates, a full storage, changes to the date, time or time zone, or turned-off notification permissions. On Android, allowing "on-time reminders" in the App helps, but cannot remove every cause.

If missing a medicine could seriously harm you, use more than one way to remember it: for example a pill organiser, an alarm clock, a family member or a carer.

## Location and hospitals

- Your location is worked out by your phone and may be missing or wrong, especially indoors.
- The hospital list comes from OpenStreetMap, a free map made by volunteers. It may be incomplete or out of date, and a place listed may not have an emergency unit or may be closed. Distances are in a straight line, not by road.
- In an emergency, call **112** or **10177** rather than travelling to a hospital on your own if you are unwell.

## Read aloud

The read-aloud feature uses your phone's built-in voice. It can mispronounce names of medicines, doctors and places. If something sounds unclear, read the screen or ask someone to help.
