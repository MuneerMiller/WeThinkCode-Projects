# Accessibility statement

Version {{legal_version}} · Effective {{effective_date}}

Elderly Care is made for older people, so being easy to see, hear and use is part of its purpose. We aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA where they apply to a mobile app.

## What the App does to help

- **Large, clear text.** The App uses Lexend, a typeface designed to be easy to read, at larger sizes than usual. It follows your phone's text size setting, and you can make text bigger still under My profile, "Text size".
- **Big buttons.** Buttons and other things you tap are at least 48 by 48 points, most are larger, and important buttons stay in the same place at the bottom of the screen.
- **Colour and contrast.** Text and buttons are chosen for strong contrast. Colour is never the only way information is shown: icons and words are always used as well. A dark theme follows your phone's setting.
- **Read aloud.** A speaker button reads out today's summary, the medicine check and your Medical ID.
- **Screen readers.** The App is built to work with TalkBack on Android, VoiceOver on Apple devices and screen readers in web browsers. Buttons have spoken names, and messages are announced.
- **Keyboard.** In the web version and with a connected keyboard, the App can be used without a mouse or touch, including typing your PIN on the lock screen.
- **Plain language.** Screens use short, everyday words, and actions that delete things always ask you to confirm.
- **No time limits.** Nothing in the App disappears before you have had time to act, except short confirmation messages, which are also announced to screen readers.

## Known limitations

- The App has been tested with automated accessibility checks and screen-reader labels in a web browser, but not yet with TalkBack and VoiceOver on a wide range of real phones.
- The read-aloud voice depends on your phone and may mispronounce some medicine or place names.
- The chart in the health tracker is described in words for screen readers, but its individual points can only be explored by touch.
- Documents you photograph are images, so a screen reader can read their names but not their contents.
- Some screens opened in other apps (Phone, Messages, Maps, Jitsi Meet, your share sheet) are outside our control.
